\lessontitle{10}{Ordered Combinatorial}

\section{This lesson}

\begin{frame}{This lesson}
Lesson 09 was bits. A tour is not a bit mask: one-point crossover duplicates cities. Ordered crossover is the operator that stays legal.

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A legal ordered chromosome contains each required member exactly once.
    \item Ordinary one-point crossover usually duplicates and loses members.
    \item A valid GA result still needs a baseline (nearest neighbour; a budget).
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ================= team_01_the_roster =================
\begin{frame}{Two constrained permutation models}
For a tour \(\pi\), with \(\pi_{n+1}=\pi_1\),
\[
L(\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d(\pi_i,\pi_{i+1}).
\]
OX1 and inversion preserve membership and uniqueness. A legal roster likewise
uses distinct identifiers in role-specific slots, obeys its budget, and
maximizes total skill. These results certify legality and compare baselines;
they do not prove global optimality.
\end{frame}
